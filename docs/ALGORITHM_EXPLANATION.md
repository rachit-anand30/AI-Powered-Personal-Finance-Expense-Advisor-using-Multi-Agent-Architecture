# 🧮 Algorithms and Search Strategy

What each agent computes, why that method was chosen, and where to find it.

---

## 1. Expense classification: NLP + machine learning
**File:** `backend/services/ml_service.py`, used by `agents/expense_analyzer_agent.py`

**Pipeline:**
1. **Normalisation:** lower-case, strip UPI handles (`@okaxis`), reference numbers and punctuation.
   `"UPI/zomato@okhdfc/Payment"` → `"upi zomato payment"`.
2. **Features (TF-IDF):** a `FeatureUnion` of
   - word 1–2-grams, which capture phrases like "mutual fund" and "electricity bill";
   - character 2–5-grams (`char_wb`), which make the model robust to typos and unseen brands:
     `"SWIGY"` still shares most n-grams with `"swiggy"`.
3. **Classifier:** multinomial **Logistic Regression** (C=10). It gives calibrated probabilities,
   which the hybrid rule below relies on.

**Hybrid decision rule:**

| ML probability | Keyword rule matches? | Result |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.55 | – | ML label |
| < 0.55 | yes | keyword label (whole-word regex, so `"business"` ≠ `"bus"`) |
| 0.30–0.55 | no | ML label (low confidence) |
| < 0.30 | no | `Others` |

**Data:** 812 labelled descriptions (`backend/data/training_transactions.csv`) generated by
`scripts/generate_training_data.py`. 165 real Indian merchants and generic phrases are rendered
through statement-style templates (`POS 4411 DMART MUMBAI`, `UPI-…`, `NEFT …`).

**Evaluation:**
| Metric | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 5-fold stratified CV | 98.7% | Optimistic: the same merchant appears in train and validation folds |
| **Hold-out, unseen merchants** | **94.0%** | 50 hand-labelled descriptions whose merchants/phrasings are *not* in training (`classifier_test_set.csv`). This is the honest number. |

The three hold-out misses ("Box8 meals", "Wow Momo", "Chai Point") are brand names with no
food-related sub-words. At runtime the keyword fallback catches some of these.

**Why not keywords alone?** The original keyword matcher put "BookMyShow" in Education ("book"),
"Business lunch" in Transport ("bus") and "Supermarket groceries" in Shopping ("market"), and could
not handle typos. All of these are now regression tests.

## 2. Anomaly detection: three-detector ensemble
**File:** `agents/anomaly_detection_agent.py`

Transaction amounts are heavily right-skewed: lots of ₹200 UPI payments and one ₹15,000 rent. A
mean/std z-score is distorted by the very outlier it is trying to detect, and Isolation Forest with
a fixed `contamination=0.1` *always* flags 10% of the data. The old version flagged rent in a
perfectly normal month.

**Pre-filter (domain knowledge):** these are never flagged
- fixed commitments: rent, EMI, loan, insurance, SIP, fees (regex on description);
- **recurring merchants**: same merchant in ≥ 2 different months, within 1.5× of its own median
  (e.g. the monthly BigBasket order);
- investments and non-positive amounts.

**Detectors:**
| Detector | Formula / method | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Modified Z-score** (Iglewicz & Hoaglin) | `M = 0.6745 (x − median) / MAD` | **Primary.** Median and MAD are not pulled by the outlier. Flag if `M > 3.5` **and** amount ≥ 5× median. |
| Z-score | `Z = (x − μ) / σ`, threshold 2.5 | Corroborating vote |
| Isolation Forest | sklearn, `contamination="auto"`, IQR fence fallback | Corroborating vote; isolates points that need few random splits |

**Confidence** = votes ÷ 3. The ₹75,000 fraud scores M = 205, Z > 2.5 and is isolated by the
forest, giving 100% confidence.
**Risk:** High if ≥ 3 flags or any flag > 50% of monthly income; Medium if any flag.

## 3. Budget planning: 50/30/20 + search
**File:** `agents/budget_planning_agent.py`

### 3.1 Allocation
Needs 50% / Wants 30% / Savings 20% of income. A savings goal above 20% is taken out of Wants.
Spending is mapped as Needs (Bills, Healthcare, Education), Wants (Shopping, Entertainment, Others),
and Food and Transport split 50/50.

### 3.2 Health score (0–100)
```
score = 50 · clamp(actual_savings / savings_target, 0, 1)
      + 25 · min(1, needs_budget / needs_spent)²
      + 25 · min(1, wants_budget / wants_spent)²
```
Savings carries half the weight, because a budget that saves nothing is not healthy. The squared
terms make large overspends hurt more than small ones. Example: ₹20k income with ₹18.5k spent
scores **59 (High risk)**. The old formula gave this 93.

### 3.3 Search strategy: greedy best-first search over cuts
**Problem formulation:**
| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| State | number of 5% cut-steps applied to each category, e.g. `{Food: 2, Shopping: 3}` |
| Initial state | no cuts |
| Actions | cut one more 5% step from a category (only while under its maximum cut) |
| Goal test | total monthly savings from cuts ≥ savings gap (target − current savings) |
| Step cost / heuristic | **pain per rupee** = `pain_weight[c] × (1 + steps_already_cut[c])` |
| Constraints | max cut per category: Bills 10%, Healthcare 5%, Education 20%, Food/Transport 40%, Shopping/Entertainment 70% |

Pain weights: Shopping and Entertainment 1, Others 1.2, Food and Transport 2, Education 5,
Healthcare 8, Bills 12. A **priority queue** (`heapq`) always expands the cheapest next cut. The
increasing cost per repeated cut models diminishing willingness, so the search spreads cuts across
categories instead of zeroing one out. It terminates when the goal is reached or the frontier is
empty (`goal_reached: false`, meaning the gap can't be closed by cuts alone).

**Why greedy best-first?** The objective is separable and each category's marginal cost increases,
so always taking the cheapest marginal cut produces the cheapest set of cuts for a given amount of
savings, up to the 5% step size. This is the same argument that makes greedy optimal for the
fractional knapsack problem. A* or uniform-cost search over the full state space would expand far
more nodes for the same answer. Example: the Budget Warning scenario reaches its ₹2,499 gap in
**26 node expansions**, and rent is never touched.

## 4. Expense prediction
**File:** `agents/prediction_agent.py`

| History | Method | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 3 months | **Linear Regression** `y = m·t + b` (sklearn, `np.polyfit` fallback) | `45 + 40·R² + 2·months` (max 95) |
| 1–2 months | Moving Average of available months | `40 + 15·months` |

- Time series = monthly totals, **excluding** investment transfers and anything the Anomaly agent
  flagged.
- Trend: last month vs the previous one (±5% band = Stable).
- Risk: forecast ÷ income > 0.9 → High, > 0.7 → Medium.

Example: Lifestyle Creep (₹35.8k → 42.0k → 49.2k → 57.2k) gives a forecast of ₹63.8k for October
with R² ≈ 0.99, i.e. 93% confidence.

## 5. Investment planning
**File:** `agents/investment_agent.py`. Rule-based priority ladder:
1. **Emergency fund** = 6 × monthly expenses. 30–40% of the surplus goes here until it is full.
2. **Protection**: term and health insurance before chasing returns.
3. **SIP allocation** of the rest by risk profile:

| Profile | Chosen when | Equity / Debt / Gold | Illustrative return |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | savings rate < 10% or forecast risk High | 30 / 55 / 15 | 8% |
| Balanced | otherwise | 55 / 35 / 10 | 10% |
| Aggressive | savings rate ≥ 30%, forecast Low, fraud not High | 75 / 15 / 10 | 12% |

Projection uses the SIP future value `FV = P · ((1+r)^n − 1)/r · (1+r)`.
Investing is paused when there is a deficit. The output is labelled educational guidance, not
personalised advice.

## 6. Advisor: NLP question answering
**File:** `agents/advisor_agent.py`
1. **Intent detection:** keyword scoring over 8 intents (afford, save, invest, fraud, forecast,
   budget, category, health).
2. **Entity extraction (regex):** rupee amounts in any form (`₹30,000`, `rs 4500`, `25k`,
   `1.5 lakh`) and category mentions ("food", "uber", "netflix").
3. **Response generation:** an intent-specific template filled with numbers from the other agents.
   For example, *"Can I afford a 60k laptop?"* gives ₹60,000 ÷ ₹29,947 monthly surplus = 2.0 months.

Recommendations are generated from the data (flagged charges, deficit, savings gap, the search's
cuts, rising trend, investable surplus), ranked high / medium / low. Savings opportunities come from
regex over transaction text: streaming subscriptions and food-delivery merchants.
